# Plan: Caspase activity assay development with progranulin conditioned media (H4 cells)

Source: lab notebook entry (target PGRN; assay development; cell-based; flow; cell type H4). This plan covers the plate acquired on 16-Dec-2022. People's names, and all text written after acquisition, have been removed.

## Purpose
To determine if progranulin conditioned media can restore wild-type levels of caspase activity in H4 GRN KO cells.

## Rationale
Previous literature evidence suggests that applying exogenous progranulin to cells can restore molecular phenotypes in progranulin deficient lines. Our previous experiment with caspase activity on H4 GRN WT and KO cells showed that GRN KO lines had increased caspase activity. This difference was enhanced after campothesin challenge in both GRN WT and KO cells. In this experiment, we will determine if applying conditioned media from WT cell lines onto KO lines can reduce caspase activity closer to WT levels.

# Marks & anatomy

The quiet, considered look is a few fixed specs plus two pieces of negative space.
The data is the only thing allowed to be loud.

## Mark specs (fixed across every chart)

| Mark | Spec |
|---|---|
| Bar / column | **<= 24px thick** (cap it - never fill the slot; let the band's leftover be air); **4px rounded data-end, square at the baseline**; grows from a single baseline |
| Line | **2px**, round join/cap |
| Marker / end-dot | **>= 8px** (r >= 4), filled with the series color |
| Area fill | the series hue at **~10% opacity** (a wash, never a saturated block) |
| Gridlines / axes | one-step-off-surface gray, **hairline (1px), solid** (never dashed), recessive |

## The two spacers (white doing the separating)

- **Surface gap.** A **2px gap** in the surface color separates touching marks - every
  segment of a stacked bar, and every adjacent (touching) bar, the same width. Keep it
  one consistent width across a stack; neighbors one step apart read distinct because of
  the gap, not a stroke drawn around them.
- **Surface ring.** Dots and end-markers carry a **2px ring in the surface color**,
  so they stay legible where they cross a line or overlap each other. The ring is part
  of the mark's hover/hit target, not just spacing - see `interaction.md` (small dots
  are easy to under-size for hover).

Never draw a border around a mark to separate it. The gap and the ring are the
mechanism; a stroke adds data-weight ink that isn't data.

But the bigger finding is that the caspase stain never worked on this plate, so it can't answer the question either way. All seven deliverables are in `/task/outputs/`, and a final script check of the CSV, JSON and report formats passed.

**What I did.** All gating and statistics were done in the Cytoflow GUI, using all events: Cells (FSC-A/SSC-A polygon), then Singlets (FSC-A/FSC-H polygon), then Live (SYTOX on BL2 below 20,000), then CaspasePos (caspase stain on BL1 above 500, which is the 99.5th percentile of the unstained control G1). The per-gate counts exported from Table views match a separate all-gates export exactly. The channel names stored in the files are out of date ("AAV-GFP-GFP" is BL1, the caspase stain), so I assigned channels from the plan and the voltages in the file metadata.

**Main findings**
- **No caspase-positive population anywhere.** Every one of the 1.15 million events has a BL1 value below 830, on a scale that goes to 1,048,576. The highest camptothecin dose (10 µM) raised the wild-type caspase-positive fraction only from 0.69% to 1.71%.
- **Responding cells were probably thrown away.** Camptothecin did affect the cells: at 10 µM, live cells per well fell by 59% (WT) and about 75% (knockouts), yet fewer than 2.2% of the remaining cells stain as dead. The protocol aspirates the medium and washes with PBS before trypsinising, so detached dying cells were most likely discarded.
- **Effect of conditioned media** (caspase-positive % of live cells, all doses pooled, 8 wells each):

  | Line | No CM | 24 h CM | 48 h CM |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | WT | 1.25 | 1.11 | 1.13 |
  | E1 knockout | 5.02 | 3.93 | 2.69 |
  | E3 knockout | 6.26 | 5.57 | 6.00 |

  E1 dropped 46% with 48 h media, which is still about twice the wild-type level, and E3 did not change.
- **The knockout vs wild-type gap may not be caspase.** Knockout cells are about 24% larger by forward scatter. There are no unstained controls for the knockout lines, and within E1 the caspase-positive fraction tracks cell size. The E1 wells given 48 h media also had smaller cells. Each media type also sits in a fixed block of four columns, so media is confounded with plate position.

**Well flags**
- **B1 excluded:** the SYTOX dead-cell stain appears not to have been added. Only 0.33% of its cells are SYTOX-bright, against 10–35% in the rest of its row.
- **Seven knockout 10 µM wells kept with a note:** each has fewer than 2,000 live cells.
- **E10 kept with a note:** it is a replicate outlier.

**Verdict and next step:** the assay needs optimisation, and the recommended action is to repeat with a change. On a small pilot plate:
- Collect each well's medium and PBS wash and pool them with the trypsinised cells before staining.
- Add a validated positive control (for example staurosporine) and test a longer camptothecin time.
- Include unstained and single-stain controls for every cell line.

Re-run the conditioned-media plate only once the caspase stain gives a distinct bright population.
