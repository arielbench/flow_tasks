### Background:
Loss of function of PGRN has been shown to negatively impact neural cell health reflected by increased cell death and caspase activity.
Caspases are important mediators of the cell death pathway (apoptosis). Caspases are a group of proteases that result in cleavage of protein substrates subsequently leading to cell disassembly. For the PGRN RNAswap program we will want to measure caspase activity in iPSC derived neurons before and after adding the PGRN RNAswap constructs to ensure that PGRN protein replacement is rescuing the health of neurons. Before testing if AAV-PGRN constructs help restore neural health I will test and optimize the assay on the neurons with no AAV.

The KO wells actually had fewer caspase-positive live cells than WT, and three problems with the run mean a genotype difference can't be concluded. All seven deliverables are in `/task/outputs/`. Gating and statistics were done in Cytoflow, and the workflow is saved after the last edit.

**Results** (% caspase-positive among live single cells):

| Condition | WT | KO |
|---|---|---|
| DMSO | 32.4% | 19.9% |
| Camptothecin, 4 doses pooled (mean ± SD) | 57.0 ± 3.5% | 37.0 ± 0.5% |
| Fold over own DMSO | 1.76× | 1.85× |

- **The assay does detect caspase activation.** Camptothecin raised the caspase-positive fraction about 1.8-fold in both genotypes. All four doses (1–10 µM) gave the same response, so 4.5 h at 1 µM is already at a plateau.
- **KO had more dead cells:** in DMSO, 92.4% of KO cells were dead vs 84.7% of WT.

**Why the genotype comparison isn't reliable:**
- **One well per condition.** No statistical test is possible; the SDs above only reflect dose-to-dose spread.
- **The caspase stain was about 2-fold dimmer in every KO well.** This includes the caspase-only control (median 2,088 vs 3,675), and dead cells were 0.31–0.45× as bright as in matched WT wells. A fixed positivity cutoff therefore undercounts KO positives.
- **Very low viability:** only 4–20% of cells were alive, so the readout describes a small surviving subset. Much of the death may come from the harvest and the 30-minute staining step at 37 °C.

**Data issues:**
- **A12 (WT no treatment) clogged and is excluded.** Only 0.69% of its events arrived in the first 20 s, against 43–46% in every other well, then they came in an irregular burst. WT therefore has no untreated control, and DMSO is the baseline.
- **The caspase channel is mislabelled in the files.** BL1-A is named "AAV-GFP-Caspase-A" but carries the CellEvent caspase stain; no AAV was used in this run.
- **The six KO treatment wells and the KO caspase-only control are flagged "keep with note"** for the dimmer staining.
- **0.4–2.1% of events per well saturate side scatter.** They fall outside the cell gate and aren't analysed.

**Verdict and next step:** the assay needs optimisation, and the run should be repeated with one change: at least 3 wells per genotype for DMSO and 1 µM camptothecin only, with every well stained at the same counted cell number. The report also suggests a gentler harvest to raise viability, re-acquiring the untreated controls and lowering the side-scatter voltage slightly.

**The main judgement call is the live gate.** I set it at the unstained level (Zombie signal ≤ 600) because the Zombie-stained events scale tightly with cell size, which is the pattern of dead cells. That reading is why viability comes out so low. A looser gate would raise viability but wouldn't change the camptothecin effect within each genotype.
